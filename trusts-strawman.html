Trusts, Estates & the “Strawman”

Learn how real trusts and estates work, and how that differs from popular “strawman” and birth‑certificate theories.

What a trust really is

A trust is a legal relationship where a trustee holds and manages property for one or more beneficiaries under defined terms.

Trusts are usually created deliberately (for example, in a written trust document or will) and are governed by state trust and probate laws.

What an estate really is

An estate is the collection of property, rights, and obligations a person leaves at death.

Courts handle estates in probate, distributing assets to heirs or beneficiaries and settling debts.

Usufruct and “public trust” language

Some teachers say that the 1933 financial changes created a “usufructuary trust” where government uses your property while you retain bare ownership. They argue that “redeeming your estate” is about ending that usufruct and reclaiming full control, usually through specific forms, notices, or affidavits.

The strawman concept

The “strawman” theory claims your NAME IN ALL CAPS is a separate corporate entity used by government and banks. Proponents say you can separate yourself from this entity and avoid certain obligations by specific wording or paperwork.

Mainstream courts consistently treat this theory as incorrect: capitalization does not create or destroy a legal person, and obligations like taxes or criminal liability do not vanish because of wording games.
